## 6. Limitations and Bias

**Features it does not consider:**
- Tempo — a song's BPM is loaded from the CSV but never used in scoring, so a user who wants fast-paced music gets no benefit from that preference
- Lyrics, language, or cultural context — the system only sees numbers and labels, not what a song is actually about
- Danceability — also loaded but ignored in the scoring formula

**Genres or moods that are underrepresented:**
- The 20-song catalog is heavily weighted toward lofi, ambient, and pop — genres like hip-hop, R&B, classical, and country are missing entirely
- Moods like "sad", "romantic", or "angry" don't exist in the dataset, so users who want those will never get a mood match and rely entirely on energy/valence

**Cases where the system overfits to one preference:**
- Because mood is binary (full credit or zero), a user with a strong mood preference will always see that dominate over everything else — even if the energy or valence is a very poor match
- When we tested a conflicting profile (`energy=0.90, mood=chill`), chill low-energy songs ranked at the top because the 0.35 mood weight overrode the energy signal

**Ways the scoring might unintentionally favor some users:**
- Users who prefer chill or acoustic music are better served because those moods and genres have more songs in the catalog
- Users who prefer high-energy or electronic music have fewer matching songs, so their top results may still feel like a poor match even at high scores
- The weights (0.35, 0.25, 0.25, 0.15) were chosen manually — they assume mood matters most for everyone, which may not be true for all users
